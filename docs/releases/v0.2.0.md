**Polaris v0.2.0** adds a persistent telescope mounting calibration for phones strapped *parallel* to the tube at any angle.

## What's new
- **Mounting profiles (Telescope › Pointing model › Mounting profile)**
  - The phone only needs to lie flat on a surface parallel to the tube. Its top edge does *not* have to point along the tube.
  - A one-time calibration on 2–5 bright objects measures the tube's direction in the phone's own frame (in-plane angle plus small out-of-plane tilt) and shows per-sighting residuals.
  - Profiles are saved, named and reusable. Later sessions work immediately, with an optional one-star **sync** that removes the day's compass error and checks whether the phone has shifted on its mount.
  - Pointing stays correct however the tube or phone is rolled (e.g. when rotating the star diagonal).
- **Heading source**: *Compass* (absolute, sensitive to metal and magnets) or *Gyro only* (immune to magnetic interference, drifts slowly, synced on a star; the app shows time since the last sync).
- Sightings and syncs average 1.5 s of sensor data and are rejected if the telescope moved.
- **Simple mode** (v0.1.0 behaviour) is unchanged and remains available.
- New document: `docs/TELESCOPE_MOUNTING.md` (model, calibration procedure, error sources, expected accuracy).

## Expected accuracy
Altitude ±0.5–1.5°. Azimuth ±1–3° near the sync object after a session sync (compass), ±3–10° without a sync. Gyro mode is ±1–2° right after a sync, then drifts a few degrees per 10–30 minutes. This is not plate solving.

## Known limitations
- Not yet field-tested on a real telescope. Accuracy figures are engineering estimates for consumer phone IMUs.
- The profile is only valid while the phone stays in the same position on the tube.
- Mount flexure and non-level mounts are not modelled.

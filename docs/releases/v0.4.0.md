**Polaris v0.4.0** makes Phone plane navigation pinpoint-accurate.

## What's new
- **Phone plane mode now uses the plane's most vertical line.** Being "in the plane" is no longer enough: the object must lie on the steepest line across the phone's tilted plane (world-up projected onto the plane; altitude = 90° − the phone's tilt). Together these give a single pointing direction.
- Spinning the phone within its plane still doesn't matter. Only the plane's tilt does.
- New readout: **pinpoint error** (angle between the vertical line and the object). It reads "on target" within 1°.
- Clear instructions: "Turn left 84°, make the phone flatter 16°" (turn about the vertical, plus steeper or flatter).
- The off-plane angle, its meter and the "raise/lower the … edge" hint remain as a secondary aid.
- The diagram now draws the vertical line (dashed) together with the target arrow and the edge to tilt.
- Shows where the plane's vertical line points (azimuth/altitude).
- A phone lying level (no vertical line) is detected and you're asked to tilt it.

## Known limitations
- Not yet field-tested on a real phone and telescope. The math is verified by unit tests (including "following the instructions reaches the target") and simulated sensor input.
- The vertical line is undefined when the phone lies level, and its azimuth becomes sensitive when the phone is nearly upright (objects near the zenith). Camera mode is better there.
- Azimuth accuracy depends on the compass (2–15° near metal) or on gyro drift. A star sync helps.
